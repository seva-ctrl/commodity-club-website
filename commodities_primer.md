# Commodities Trading: A Primer

A practical overview of the major commodity markets: what they are, who trades them, and how prices are benchmarked.

---

## 1. Crude Oil

Crude oil is raw petroleum pulled out of the ground before it gets refined into anything usable. It's the world's most traded commodity, and nearly every economy depends on it. Prices move on geopolitics, OPEC production decisions, storage levels, and global demand. Traders deal in physical barrels as well as financial contracts that let buyers and sellers lock in prices without ever touching the oil itself.

Two grades dominate global pricing: WTI (West Texas Intermediate), the U.S. benchmark, and Brent, which prices most crude from the Atlantic Basin and North Sea. The gap between them is itself a tradeable instrument that reflects regional supply and demand at any given moment.

**Major Players**

- *Producers:* Saudi Aramco, ExxonMobil, Shell, BP, TotalEnergies, Chevron, Rosneft, ADNOC
- *Trading Houses:* Vitol, Trafigura, Glencore, Gunvor, Mercuria
- *Banks:* Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan
- *End-Users:* Refineries, Airlines, Shipping Companies

**Key Benchmarks and Instruments**

- WTI Crude (NYMEX: CL)
- Brent Crude (ICE: B)
- Dubai/Oman
- WTI-Brent Spread
- CMA Swaps (Calendar Month Average)
- CFD Swaps (Contract for Difference)

## 2. Refined Products

Crude oil does nothing on its own. Refineries crack it into usable products: gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, heating oil, fuel oil, naphtha. Each has its own supply chain and price logic. Refinery margins, called the "crack spread," measure the difference between what a refinery pays for crude and what it earns selling products, and they're one of the most watched numbers in the oil market.

Real demand tends to show up in products before it shows up in crude. A cold winter tightens heating oil. Summer driving lifts gasoline. Jet fuel demand fell off a cliff in 2020 well before crude markets fully processed it. If you want exposure to where oil actually gets consumed, this is the market.

**Major Players**

- *Producers/Refiners:* Valero, Marathon Petroleum, Phillips 66, Neste, Reliance Industries, PetroChina
- *Trading Houses:* Vitol, Trafigura, Gunvor, Freepoint Commodities
- *End-Users:* Airlines, Trucking and Logistics Companies, Fuel Distributors, Petrochemical Plants

**Key Benchmarks and Instruments**

- RBOB Gasoline (NYMEX: RB)
- ULSD / Heating Oil (NYMEX: HO)
- Gasoil (ICE: G)
- Jet Fuel / Jet Kerosene
- Crack Spreads (3-2-1)
- Product Swaps

## 3. Natural Gas

Natural gas heats buildings, generates electricity, and feeds industrial and petrochemical processes. Unlike oil, it's expensive and difficult to transport: you either pipe it or liquefy it (LNG) and ship it. That constraint keeps gas markets regional in a way oil markets are not. U.S. prices can be completely disconnected from European or Asian prices depending on infrastructure and LNG flow.

Seasonality drives everything. Cold winters and hot summers create demand spikes that can move prices dramatically in a short window. Storage levels are tracked obsessively, since a deficit heading into winter can push prices to multiples of where they started the year. The 2022 European energy crisis accelerated the LNG buildout globally, gradually tying what were previously isolated regional markets together.

**Major Players**

- *Producers:* EQT, Chesapeake Energy, ConocoPhillips, Shell, QatarEnergy, Novatek
- *Trading Houses:* Shell Trading, Vitol, Cheniere Energy, Trafigura
- *Banks:* Goldman Sachs, Citigroup
- *End-Users:* Utilities, Industrial Manufacturers, LNG Importers

**Key Benchmarks and Instruments**

- Henry Hub (NYMEX: NG)
- TTF (ICE)
- JKM (Japan Korea Marker)
- NBP (National Balancing Point)
- Basis Swaps
- Seasonal Spreads

## 4. Power / Electricity

Power is unlike every other commodity because you can't store it at scale. Electricity has to be generated and consumed at the same instant. That balancing requirement produces some of the most extreme price swings in any market: prices can go negative overnight when wind farms are producing and nobody needs the output, then spike to extraordinary levels during a heatwave when grid capacity gets overwhelmed.

Traders deal in everything from day-ahead auctions to long-dated financial contracts that let utilities and large industrial consumers hedge years out. Renewable growth has changed how these markets behave. Wind and solar add cheap but unpredictable supply, which tends to create more volatility rather than less.

**Major Players**

- *Generators:* NextEra Energy, Duke Energy, Enel, RWE, Orsted, EDF
- *Trading Houses:* Vitol, Trafigura, Shell Energy
- *Specialized Power Traders:* Castleton Commodities, Hartree Partners, Macquarie Energy
- *End-Users:* Large Industrials, Municipalities, Data Centers, Aluminum Smelters

**Key Benchmarks and Instruments**

- PJM
- ERCOT
- CAISO
- EEX (European Energy Exchange)
- Day-Ahead and Real-Time Markets
- Spark Spreads
- Dark Spreads
- RECs (Renewable Energy Certificates)

## 5. Metals

Metals fall into two categories: base metals (copper, aluminum, zinc, nickel, lead, tin) and precious metals (gold, silver, platinum, palladium). Base metals are industrial, going into buildings, cars, wiring, batteries, and infrastructure. Precious metals sit between industrial use and financial demand, with gold functioning as a store of value and a hedge against currency weakness in a way the others don't.

Copper has earned the nickname "Dr. Copper" because its price tracks global economic activity fairly well. Strong construction and manufacturing usually means strong copper demand. The energy transition has added another layer: EVs and grid buildout consume enormous amounts of copper, nickel, and cobalt, creating structural demand that runs on top of the normal economic cycle.

**Major Players**

- *Miners/Producers:* BHP, Rio Tinto, Glencore, Freeport-McMoRan, Vale, Norilsk Nickel, Barrick Gold, Newmont
- *Trading Houses:* Glencore, Trafigura, IXM, Gerald Metals
- *Banks:* ICBC Standard Bank, Goldman Sachs, HSBC
- *End-Users:* Auto Manufacturers, Construction Companies, Wire and Cable Producers, Electronics Manufacturers

**Key Benchmarks and Instruments**

- LME Copper (CA), Aluminum (AH), Nickel, Zinc
- COMEX Gold (GC), Silver (SI), Copper (HG)
- Gold Spot (XAUUSD)
- TCs/RCs (Treatment and Refining Charges)
- Metal Swaps
- Cash vs. 3-Month Spreads

## 6. Agriculture

Ag commodities cover grains (corn, wheat, soybeans, rice), softs (sugar, coffee, cocoa, cotton), oilseeds, and livestock. These markets predate nearly everything else in commodity trading. Exchanges were originally built so farmers and processors could manage price risk before crops were even harvested. Weather is the main driver: a drought in Brazil, a flood in the Midwest, or a frost in Vietnam can move coffee or soy 20% in a week.

What separates ag from other commodities is the crop cycle. Supply resets every harvest. A bad year sends prices to multi-year highs; one good growing season can knock them back down just as fast. Geopolitics matters here too. Ukraine and Russia together account for a substantial share of global wheat exports, which is why grain prices hit record levels within days of the 2022 invasion.

**Major Players**

- *Merchants (the "ABCD" firms):* ADM, Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus
- *Other Traders:* Viterra, COFCO
- *End-Users:* Flour Mills, Food Manufacturers, Animal Feed Producers, Ethanol Plants, Confectionery Companies
- *Financial Players:* Commodity Trading Advisors (CTAs), Hedge Funds

**Key Benchmarks and Instruments**

- CBOT Corn (C), Soybeans (S), Wheat (W)
- ICE Sugar No. 11 (SB), Cotton (CT), Cocoa (CC), Coffee (KC)
- MATIF Milling Wheat
- Basis
- Soybean Crush Spread
- Weather Derivatives
